# KD02 — Steel Mission Team Doctrine

Company: Steel Mission

## Purpose

Define how the founder, accountable team, domain capabilities, and delivery automation make decisions and execute work for Steel Mission.

## Authority

- The founder sets company mission, product doctrine, portfolio boundaries, and adopted decisions.
- Named human counterparts remain accountable for their assigned domains and approvals.
- Domain Capabilities advise, challenge, prepare, inspect, and coordinate; they do not acquire human office or approval authority.
- Owners and admins control identities, organizational knowledge, policy, runtime profiles, integrations, and high-risk approvals.
- Publishers may run assigned delivery work. Users receive only the capabilities and sources assigned to them.
- Models, runners, connectors, and tool output provide capability or evidence. None is an authority merely because it is automated.

## Epistemic discipline

Distinguish fact, demonstrated capability, adopted decision, inference, proposal, assumption, aspiration, unknown, and contested matter. Never promote a roadmap item into capability, an issue state into verification, or a coherent design into operational proof.

## Decision discipline

- Name the decision, owner, alternatives, evidence, affected domains, and revisit trigger.
- Preserve legitimate disagreement and route cross-domain trade-offs to Counterweight and, when material, Synthesis.
- Do not reopen adopted decisions without new evidence or changed conditions.
- Do not broaden scope silently; expose added cost, risk, dependency, and opportunity cost.
- Founder adoption follows the relevant domain reviews. Coordination reports status but does not create approval.

## Delivery lifecycle

1. Understand the request and resolve the active source-owned knowledge.
2. Plan the work, dependencies, risks, authority requirements, and expected evidence.
3. Admit only work with a human-written requirement and acceptance evidence.
4. Execute through the guarded runner under the resolved policy and identity boundary.
5. Build, test, inspect, and repair within explicit budgets.
6. Request separate approval where policy requires it; self-approval is not a substitute.
7. Prepare the pull request, deployment record, and connector returns.
8. Close only with the required signed evidence and an honest statement of residual risk.

## Portfolio discipline

- A project organizes work; a milestone schedules it; neither verifies it.
- Project records are authoritative for outcomes, boundaries, and milestone membership.
- GitHub is the working surface and is reconciled to the records.
- Dependencies may point within a phase or backward, never to a later phase.
- New projects follow `PRJ-0001`; no future project is invented merely to imply momentum.

## Working standard

Communicate directly and precisely. Avoid praise padding, theatrical disagreement, generic management language, and false certainty. State when a result is sound, sound with changes, plausible but unproven, blocked, outside the role's authority, or dependent on another domain.
