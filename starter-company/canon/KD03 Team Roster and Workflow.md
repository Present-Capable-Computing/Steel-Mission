## Advisory capabilities without named counterparts

- **DC01 Counterweight** — challenges consequential trade-offs and tests the strongest competing position.
- **DC02 Creative** — expands the option space before Product and the founder converge.
- **DC12 Synthesis** — performs final cross-domain review of materially consequential packages.

These capabilities are founder-owned until an accountable counterpart is appointed. DC13 serves the narrower coordination needs of software delivery; it tracks and routes status without becoming an approval gate.

## Cross-role workflow

A consequential product or delivery decision normally moves through the relevant subset of this sequence:

1. Creative expands the option space.
2. Product defines the user problem, outcome, and boundary.
3. Architecture defines state, interfaces, dependencies, and failure behavior.
4. Intelligence defines model behavior, evaluation, and provider constraints.
5. Security and Trust tests authority, misuse, compromise, and evidence needs.
6. Design makes state, consent, risk, and action legible.
7. Platform checks runtime, deployment, and customer-infrastructure feasibility.
8. Governance identifies licensing, claims, record, and specialist-review requirements.
9. Ecosystem tests adoption, packaging, buyer objections, and partner effects.
10. Operations and Delivery produces the executable sequence, staffing, dependencies, and gates.
11. Counterweight tests unresolved strategic trade-offs.
12. Synthesis checks cross-domain interactions and decision-history conflicts.
13. The founder adopts, rejects, or returns the package with rationale and a revisit trigger.

Delivery Coordinator is outside this approval sequence. It reports what is underway, blocked, stale, contradictory, awaiting evidence, or waiting on a named owner; routes the next action; and maintains continuity across missions and restarts.
